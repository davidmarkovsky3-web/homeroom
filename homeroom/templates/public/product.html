Scheduling

Administrators define the bell schedule once — named periods with start and end times — then assign each section a period, a room, a teacher and the rotation days it meets on (A, B, or both). Homeroom refuses to double-book a student into two sections that share a period and a rotation day.

The Schedule tab renders the full Monday–Friday grid, greying out sections that don't meet on that day's rotation. The Responsive Schedule tab collapses to a single day designed for a phone: a large "right now" card, a live countdown to the bell, and a scrollable timeline of the rest of the day. It refreshes itself without a page reload.

  • Named bell periods with real start and end times
  • A/B rotation days and no-school days on the calendar
  • Conflict detection at enrollment time
  • Printable week grids for counselor meetings

Attendance

Teachers take attendance per section, per day, with four states: present, absent, tardy and excused. The roster defaults everyone to present, so a normal day is two taps. Notes attach to individual records for parent calls and nurse passes.

Students see their own record — a term attendance rate, a per-course breakdown and a recent history. Administrators get a daily overview: the school-wide rate, who was marked absent or tardy, which sections still haven't submitted, and a chronic-absence list for the term.

Course selection

Administrators open a selection window with a date range and a required number of choices. For each choice a student submits a 1st pick and an optional 2nd pick alternate, so counselors have a fallback the moment a section fills or a period conflicts.

Homeroom validates as the student submits: no duplicate first picks, no picking the same course as both your 1st and 2nd, and every required slot filled before the form is accepted. Counselors then approve or deny request by request; approving a request seats the student automatically unless the section is already full.

Calendar

One month grid carries the whole school year: which rotation day each date is, which dates have no school, and every assembly, exam, deadline and game. Events can be scoped to a single course or a single grade level, so a student's calendar shows their unit tests alongside the school-wide dates without the noise of everyone else's.

Teachers create events for their own sections. Administrators create anything and set the rotation for any date.
